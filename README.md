# Brass & Aether

The Windows playtest includes Easy, Medium, and Hard expeditions in Brass Frontier, Glass Foundry, Crown Relay, and Moonwreck Docks, plus a separate practice arena. Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7.2 and press F5.

## Playtest

From the title screen, choose **New Expedition**, select **Brass Frontier**, and start on **Easy** to draft a hero, relic, and starting tile. You can enter an optional seed on the difficulty screen. Win the opening fight, pick rewards, then travel a seeded route through battles, elites, shops, events, and a rest site. Health, relics, gold, and tile layouts carry between stops. The game saves between fights and after loadout changes. **Continue** picks up from the saved checkpoint.

**Save slots** switches among three independent profiles. **New Game** starts with fresh progress in the chosen slot. **New Expedition** uses the active slot's existing unlocks and Aether Marks. Earlier saves open in Slot 1.

Each class has 18 Commons and eight Uncommons in its combat reward deck. Use **View class deck** on a hero or recruit card to inspect each tile and the class's signature Uncommon. After the victory animation, choose or skip a tile directly in the arena for each hero in party order. Rerolls start at 5 gold and double in price with each use until the next act. Tiles go to that hero's timeline when space allows, then to shared storage. The expedition saves after each pick. After the party takes its boss rewards, choose one of two starting loadouts for the new recruit. Each gives them their class starter and a second tile.

In **Brass Frontier Medium and Hard**, the lead hero's first tile draft offers their familiar class starter, a shared or relic-linked tile, and one of two alternate attacks from their class deck. The seed determines which alternate appears. Starshot can see Backdraft Bolt or Parallax Shot, and Lancer can see Phalanx Charge or Recoil Thrust. Take an alternate and the first combat draft favors new tiles while still offering a third copy when you can combine it.

In **Brass Frontier Medium**, the opening combat reward offers Warden a Guard or knockback direction, Hexwright a splash or protection direction, and Starshot a precision or mobile firing direction. Holding one of those tiles offers a complementary tile in a later Act I draft. You can still take other tiles and change direction as the run develops.

Each new Brass Frontier expedition has one of three optional rewards at an Act I mystery node, including on Easy. **Salvage Bench** copies a Common you own. **Traveling Smith** upgrades an eligible tile for free. **Class Cache** offers an Uncommon from your hero's deck after you choose a category.

The Bellmaker alternates a close shockwave and a shot at the position it marked during its windup. On Easy, the Tollkeeper may fight alone or with a Repair Drone. Separate them, hit the drone during its windup, or destroy it first.

Enemies fill a charge gauge above their heads before an attack. Tiles show damage and fire on their right edge. Each hero's timeline shows a portrait, live health, and targeting buttons. The left and right arrows select nearest and farthest. The solid and hollow hearts select highest and lowest HP. The **All** buttons apply the same rule to compatible tiles on the lane.

Tiles pulse in their hero's color when they fire. Critical hits appear as larger gold damage numbers. Direct tile hits have a 5% chance to deal 1.5 times damage before Guard. Starshot's ranged attacks have a 15% chance, rising to 30% beyond 210 units. A ranged critical hit can trigger a backstep once each loop.

Attacks have a ready pose and a release effect that follows their actual timing. Bash lunges, Slam drives down into a ripple, Shot recoils, Pull draws a tether, and Blink leaves a trail between its start and landing point. Tile upgrades vary the effect. Enemy windups and impacts distinguish moves such as charges, volleys, shields, and heals.

Tile rarity runs gray, green, blue, purple, and gold from Common to Legendary. Hero badges identify who has a tile equipped or who receives a reward. In shared storage, a signature tile's badge identifies its class of origin.

Open **Combat stats** during or after a fight to rank tiles by damage, assists, kills, or healing. Tiles receive one assist per enemy they damage or move before a different tile kills it. **Retry in Practice** replays the original encounter with an editable copy of your equipment and pre-fight health.

Defeat the Tollkeeper on Easy to bank Aether Marks and unlock **Medium**. Open the **Aether Chart** from the title or after a run to inspect prerequisites and spend Marks on permanent upgrades between expeditions.

Warden, Hexwright, Apothecary, Starshot, and Duelist are available from the start. In **Aether Chart → Class roster**, spend Marks between expeditions to unlock the other seven classes for future hero and recruit drafts. After your first Brass Frontier clear, **Choose from roster** on the opening draft lets you pick any class you have unlocked. Starter relic and tile drafts follow either choice.

Clearing an expedition unlocks two **Class mastery** options for each hero in your party. In a later run, choose one while equipping that class. It adds a bonus alongside the original passive, and the gold star next to the hero's name shows the active mastery.

Unlock **Tile Enchantments** on the Aether Chart after **Gravity Hook**. Brass Frontier then drafts three options after the opening reward and again after both heroes resolve their first regular combat rewards following recruitment. Options vary by seed and favor tiles you already own. The pool includes **Guarded**, **Longshot**, **Splash**, **Piercing**, **Embered**, and **Mending**. Select a tile with **Tile storage** open to attach or move either enchantment between fights. Each tile can carry one enchantment, marked with a badge. Compatible forge upgrades keep an enchantment attached. A discard or incompatible upgrade frees it for another tile.

Combine any three identical tiles below Legendary to choose between two upgrades at the next rarity. Touching copies offer a combine on the timeline or in storage. For copies spread across rows or timelines, select one with **Tile storage** open and choose **Combine 3**. A combat reward can count as the third copy for its hero using their timeline and shared storage. This works even when the reward tile itself would not fit.

A camp can advance one eligible tile instead of healing the party. The **Aether Chart** unlocks camp upgrades and direct reward drops for Blink, Shot, and Guard. **Wayfarer's Pin** adds a starter relic choice that grants each hero 6 Guard on their first successful teleport each loop. It can also appear in shops and elite rewards.

**Marksman's Arsenal** unlocks precision fire and shots that hit clustered enemies. Precision tiles let heroes keep their distance when the lane has no melee attacks. **Bastion Workshop** unlocks counters fueled by Guard and tiles that shield vulnerable allies. Shops offer one specialist upgrade from the unlocked families, from Uncommon on the first leg to Rare on the second and Epic on the third. **Pathfinder's Chart** adds seeded mystery encounters with gold wagers, relic brokers, and optional elite bounties.

The starter relic draft rotates build options. **Overflow Chalice** converts excess Guard into a little healing, **Stepped Gear** rewards a quick hit directly after a wide tile, and **Mercy Seal** grants Guard when you hit a weakened enemy. **Towline Knot**, **Bridgeplate**, and **Brass Metronome** also appear. Relic icons flash when they trigger. Battle rewards reserve a slot for an owned tile when possible.

Fresh expeditions can draft moves such as one-beat strikes, shots that create distance, and marks that any hero can spend on the next hit. The **Combat tiles** picker in Arena Practice includes the class expansion and all 24 Arsenal tiles.

Your first elite victory offers a relic draft before the tile rewards, which include each hero's signature Uncommon. **Switchback Lens** rewards changing targets, **Longshot Prism** boosts a hit from far away, and **Twin Bell** rewards two heroes for hitting the same enemy on the same beat. Twin Bell enters the pool once two heroes are alive. **Afterimage**, **Barrage Rack**, and **Siege Fuse** also appear and can add a compatible tile to the reward. Battle Review records the tile and beat that triggered each effect.

All twelve classes have two signature choices at Rare, Epic, and Legendary. If your lead takes their Uncommon signature in Leg 1, the next regular win offers a Rare. The gates into Legs 2 and 3 offer a Rare to the new recruit. The Leg 2 gate also offers one to the lead if they have yet to see one there or in Leg 1. Later elite wins can offer the next rarity up to Epic. Try the choices in **Arena Practice → Combat tiles → Signature upgrades**.

Enemy encounters rotate from the opening fight onward in new expeditions. Each act adds regional threats, and elites draw from separate formations. Select a fight to enter the arena directly. The cards on the right show what each enemy does. Crown War Drummers speed up nearby attacks, Glass Mirrorbacks reflect the first hit during their stance, and two separate tile hits can disarm a Powder Runner's fuse. Continue restores your saved encounter.

One optional Act I battle in each region is marked **Wave** on the route map. The arena marks where a reinforcement will arrive on beat 7 or 9 of the first loop.

Medium starts solo. After the Act I boss, take a Legendary tile into your build and recruit a second hero. Both lanes fire on the same beat clock. Push or pull the Gilded Marshal, then hit with the other hero within three beats to shatter its plate and interrupt it. Direct hits also wear down the plating, as does getting the whole crew through a sweep without losing HP. Defeating the Marshal unlocks Brass Frontier Hard and Glass Foundry Easy. Each hero can take a Legendary trophy after the final boss.

In Brass Frontier Medium and Hard, the Act I boss offers a relic draft after the Legendary pick. The Tollkeeper offers Signet, Bastion Medal, Guild Ledger, and **Repair Kit**, which can heal a hero after a full block. The Bellmaker offers **Clapper**, **Long Echo**, and **Resonant Plate** to reward support tiles, ranged attacks, and wide tiles respectively.

Glass Foundry Easy is a solo route that ends at the Crucible Twins or the **Refraction Engine**. The Engine locks a beam onto a marked line. Move off the line, displace the Engine during the windup, or time Guard for the hit. Both bosses award Aether Marks and unlock Glass Foundry Medium.

Glass Foundry Medium starts solo. After the Act I boss, take a Legendary tile and recruit a second hero for the next leg. The Prism Warden pressures the hero with the most ranged damage and the hero with the lowest current HP. Push or pull to open its ward, then hit it with the other hero within three beats. A clear records the unlocks for Glass Foundry Hard and Crown Relay Easy.

Glass Foundry Hard adds the Tempering Causeway and a third hero after the Prism Warden. When the Tempered Colossus charges Forge Sweep, push or pull it with one hero, crack its guard with a hit from a second hero, and shatter it with a hit from the third. Shattering the guard cancels Forge Sweep for the rest of the fight.

Crown Relay Easy is a solo expedition through the skybridge outposts. Relay Guards shield nearby allies. Defeat the guard or pull one enemy away from the other to break the link. Time a Bash during the Relay Captain's windup to interrupt the charged strike. The first clear records the unlock for Crown Relay Medium.

Crown Relay Medium starts solo. Take a Legendary tile after defeating the Relay Captain and recruit a second hero for the High Relay Gate. While the Relay Marshal charges, separate the pair with one hero, then Bash the Marshal with your other hero before the link returns. A clear earns the unlocks for Crown Relay Hard and Moonwreck Docks Easy.

Crown Relay Hard adds the Crown Causeway and a third hero after the Relay Marshal. During Crown Charge, assign different heroes to break the Left and Right Guards' links. Bash the Crown Regent with your third hero while both links are down. Each link has its own reconnect timer. Defeating a guard removes its link from the fight.

Moonwreck Docks Easy is a solo expedition through a wrecked harbor. Winch Operators pull Dock Bruisers into striking range. Target the Operator or push the Bruiser away before it swings. The Wreckmaster pulls your hero in with a harpoon before slamming the area around it. Bash it after the pull to move the slam out of reach. A clear earns the Moonwreck Docks Medium unlock.

Moonwreck Docks Medium adds Breakwater Gate and a second hero after the Wreckmaster. The Tidekeeper harpoons both heroes and winds up Breakwater Slam. Push it with one hero to break the anchor, then push or pull it with the other hero before the slam lands. A clear earns the Moonwreck Docks Hard unlock.

Moonwreck Docks Hard adds Blackwater Gate and a third hero after the Tidekeeper. The Drowned Admiral reels the party in before charging Undertow Sweep. Break its anchor with a push, reverse the undertow with a pull from a second hero, then interrupt the sweep with a push from the third. Stagger the tiles across all three timelines so they finish in that order before the sweep lands.

Brass Frontier Hard has three legs. Recruit after the Tollkeeper and the Gilded Marshal. When the Iron Regent raises its seals, land damaging hits from three different heroes within four beats to break them.

## Arena practice

1. Start with **Two heroes**. The preset schedules Hexwright's **Pull · Farthest** at beat 4 and Warden's **Bash · Nearest** at beat 6. Start the battle and watch the tether bring the distant clockwork within striking distance.
2. **Reset fight**, change Pull's rule to **Nearest**, and compare the target and follow-up. Each tile previews its target in setup and chooses again when it fires.
3. Open **Tile storage** above the timeline. Drag tiles in either direction while the popup is open. Neighbors shift in the preview if they fit. You can also select a tile and click a clear starting beat or storage cell. Use the popup to discard a selected tile.
4. Try **Solo hero**, 2× speed, or a layout using Blink. Reset returns to setup and retains the current layout. Changing fixtures loads that fixture's preset.
5. Open **Tile storage** to find three touching Bash tiles. Use **Combine → Yes**, inspect any rarity, then confirm one of the two upgrades offered at the next rarity. Three four-beat Slams fit together on a full timeline. In an expedition, a camp can also advance a single Slam if the upgrade fits.
6. Choose **Forge practice** from the fixture picker to start with three Epic Sunwalls on Warden's lane. Combine them into either Legendary, then try the result in battle or move it into storage.
7. Choose **Blink forge practice** to inspect the full Blink family and try its teleport upgrades without spending Aether Marks.
8. Choose **Shot and Guard practice** to forge ranged and defensive tiles and try them in the arena.

The simulation runs at 60 ticks per second, with 21 ticks per beat and 12 beats per loop. Each tile fires at its right edge, including at beat 12 before the playhead wraps. Same-tick activations run in hero order. The arena is continuous and uses scripted movement and separation, capped displacement, and checked teleport destinations.

The practice palette supplies unlimited base tiles. Switching practice fixtures resets their inventory.

## Build

With matching Godot export templates installed, create a `build` folder and export **Windows Desktop**. The preset embeds the game data in the x86-64 executable.

Run the scripts under `tests/` with Godot's `--headless --script` options to check practice combat, setup controls, and the Frontier run.

## Desktop launcher

On Windows, `tools/Install-DesktopShortcut.ps1` installs the launcher in `%USERPROFILE%\Games\Brass Aether` and creates the **Brass & Aether** desktop shortcut. With the game closed, the shortcut checks GitHub `main` and replaces `Brass & Aether.exe` in that folder after a successful build and startup check. If the update fails or you're offline, the launcher opens the installed copy. Opening the shortcut while the game is running brings it to the foreground. The updater uses Git, Godot 4.7.2, and the matching Windows release template.
